Consent Form Summary:

This research study at the National Cancer Institute aims to determine if a new imaging scan, called 18F-DCFBC PET/CT, can accurately identify prostate cancer. Up to 125 men with newly diagnosed, recurring, or spread prostate cancer will participate. If you join, you’ll have blood drawn, a physical exam, and then receive an IV injection of the imaging agent followed by several PET/CT scans over a few hours. Some participants may also have MRI or additional PET/CT scans to evaluate bone disease. Potential risks include pain or bruising from the IV, allergic reactions, and a small amount of radiation exposure – comparable to yearly background radiation. There are no direct benefits to you, but the study may help future cancer patients. Participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without affecting your standard care. Your medical information will be kept private, though some details may be shared for safety and quality assurance. No payment is provided, but travel reimbursement may be available. If you experience a research-related injury, short-term care will be provided, but long-term care or financial compensation is not guaranteed.
