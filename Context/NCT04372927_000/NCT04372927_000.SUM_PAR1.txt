Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to improve treatment for stage III non-small cell lung cancer by testing a combination of immunotherapy (durvalumab), chemotherapy, and radiation. If you choose to participate, you’ll attend about 60 clinic visits over two years, including imaging, six weeks of daily radiation with weekly chemotherapy, and immunotherapy every four weeks for two years. While there’s no guarantee of benefit, and the treatment could potentially worsen your condition, the study hopes to find better ways to control cancer and reduce side effects. You have the option of receiving standard lung cancer treatment instead. The study involves potential risks like diarrhea, lung inflammation, and liver problems, which will be closely monitored. Your medical records will be kept confidential, and participation is completely voluntary – you can withdraw at any time without penalty. There is no cost to you for research-related tests, and you won’t be paid for participation. If you experience a medical issue related to the study, treatment will be provided, but related costs will likely be billed to you or your insurance. If you’re interested, contact Dr. Jing Zeng at 206-598-4100 or the emergency number at 206-589-3300. You can also reach the Institutional Review Office at 206-667-5900 for questions about your rights.

## Your rights

- You do not have to join this study. You are free to say 'yes' or 'no'.
- If you get sick or hurt in this study, you do not lose any of your legal rights to seek payment by signing this form.
- During the study, we might learn new information that you need to know. For example, some information may affect your health or well-being. Other information might make you change your mind about being in this study. If we learn these kinds of information, we would tell you.
- If you join this study, you would not have to stay in it. You could stop at any time (even before you start). Your regular medical care would not change. You would have no penalty for stopping, but it would be better not to join the study if you think that you would change your mind later.
- If you decide to drop out, we would want you to tell the study doctor. The doctor could tell you about the effects of stopping your treatment. You and the doctor could talk about the follow-up care and testing that would help the most.
- Before you leave the study, the doctor might ask you to continue in the follow-up part of the study.

A description of this clinical trial will be available on http://www.ClinicalTrials.gov , as required by U.S. Law. This Web site will not include information that can identify you. At most, the Web site will include a summary of the results. You can search this Web site at any time.
