Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to determine the safest dose of a new drug, IPdR, when given with standard treatments – capecitabine and radiation therapy – for locally advanced rectal cancer. About 20-30 participants will receive capecitabine and radiation, along with varying doses of IPdR taken by mouth daily for about six weeks. After treatment, you will be monitored for four weeks, and then have surgery approximately 8-12 weeks later. Potential risks include side effects like diarrhea and dehydration, and the possibility that the added drug may not be as effective as usual treatment. Benefits may include shrinking or stabilizing the cancer, though this is not guaranteed. You can stop participating at any time without affecting your regular medical care. Your medical information will be kept private, though it may be shared for research purposes while protecting your identity. There are no costs to you for research-related procedures, but you are responsible for costs associated with standard care like capecitabine and radiation. You will not be paid for participating, but may contribute to future advancements in cancer treatment. If you experience an injury related to the study, your insurance may not cover it.
