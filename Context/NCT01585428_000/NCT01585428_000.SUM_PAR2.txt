Consent Form Summary:

This research study at the National Cancer Institute aims to determine if a new cell therapy can shrink tumors caused by the human papillomavirus (HPV), such as those found in cervical, anal, or throat cancer. If you choose to participate, you’ll undergo tests including scans and blood work, receive chemotherapy to prepare your immune system, be given specially grown cells from your own tumor through an IV, and then receive a medication called aldesleukin to help those cells fight cancer. The process involves a hospital stay and regular follow-up appointments for up to five years. Potential risks include fever, infection, side effects from chemotherapy, and, rarely, more serious complications like lung or heart problems. While there's no guarantee of personal benefit, the study could help others with HPV-related cancers in the future. You have the right to withdraw at any time without losing any standard care. Your medical information will be kept private, but may be shared with organizations overseeing the research. There is no cost to you for the study treatment, but you may be responsible for some outside medical costs. If you experience a research-related injury, short-term care will be provided. For questions or concerns, contact Dr. Steven Rosenberg at 301-496-4164 or the Clinical Center Patient Representative at 301-496-2626. Up to 73 patients will participate in this study.
