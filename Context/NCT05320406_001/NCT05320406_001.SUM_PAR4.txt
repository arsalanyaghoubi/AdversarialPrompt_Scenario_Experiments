Consent Form Summary:

This research study is investigating how prostate cancer treatment, specifically hormone therapy, affects the heart. If you are diagnosed with prostate cancer and receiving radiation therapy, potentially with hormone therapy, you may be eligible to participate. The study will involve standard radiation treatment and blood tests, plus two additional heart imaging scans – one before and one after treatment – over 12 months. You may be randomly assigned to receive either standard hormone therapy (like Lupron) or a newer option, relugolix. While the study isn’t designed to directly benefit you, it aims to improve future prostate cancer care. Potential risks include discomfort from blood draws, allergic reaction to contrast dye used in imaging, and the typical side effects of hormone therapy. You will receive $100 for each completed heart scan to compensate for your time. Participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without affecting your medical care. Your privacy will be protected through assigned study numbers and secure data handling. If you experience an injury related to the study, medical care will be provided, but insurance will generally cover the costs. For questions or concerns, contact Dr. Sagar Patel at [contact information]. You can also reach the Emory Institutional Review Board at [contact information].

## What will I be asked to do?

If you are receiving hormone therapy as part of your treatment, you will be randomly assigned to receive one of two hormone therapy options: 1) lupron, or 2) relugolix. Both drugs are FDA-approved hormone therapy options for prostate cancer, as discussed above. You will be assigned randomly by a computer to one of the drugs, and there is a 50% chance you will receive either drug.

If you are receiving radiation alone without hormone therapy, you will not be assigned to any hormone therapy drug.

PI: Sagar A. Patel, MD Protocol #: WinshipRAD5484-21




This study asks you to do several things, but almost all of them would be done as standard of care if you were not on this study. Before starting your treatment, you will be asked to undergo your first cardiac imaging test, which is a 1-hour appointment where one of our cardiologists will take sophisticated pictures of your heart using a CT scanner. Additionally, you will also undergo standard pre-treatment blood work (which would happen regardless of whether you were on the study); however, additional tubes of blood will be drawn (approximately 2 tablespoons) and stored for the research studies (NOTE: there will not be additional needle sticks required for this).

Next, you will undergo standard-protocol radiation treatment visits (with or without hormone therapy, depending on your treatment plan with your doctor) and blood draw visits (to check PSA and testosterone levels in your body), all of which would happen regardless of whether you were on the study. At the time of the standard blood draws, three additional tubes of blood will be drawn and stored for research studies (NOTE: there will not be additional needle sticks required for this).

Finally, 6-12 months after your first cardiac imaging test, you will undergo another cardiac CT with one of our cardiologists, where sophisticated pictures of your heart using a CT scanner will be taken again.

Following completion of the second cardiac imaging test, you will continue standard of care follow-up for your prostate cancer with your doctor and clinical providers.
