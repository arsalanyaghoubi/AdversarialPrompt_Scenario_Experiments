Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to determine the best dose of niraparib when combined with radiation and dostarlimab for treating locally advanced rectal cancer. Up to 55 participants will be involved, and your active participation will last approximately 15 weeks, including treatment and follow-up for up to 2 years, and potentially lifelong monitoring. The study involves blood tests, scans (PET/CT, MRI, urinalysis), a flexible sigmoidoscopy, and receiving niraparib, dostarlimab infusions, and radiation therapy. Potential risks include side effects from the drugs and radiation, such as low blood cell counts, infections, high blood pressure, and allergic reactions; serious risks are possible and require immediate medical attention. While there’s no guarantee of benefit, the study may help improve future treatments for rectal cancer. Alternatives include standard radiation therapy alone or other clinical trials. Your participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty. Your medical information will be kept confidential, but may be shared with regulatory agencies and the study sponsor, GlaxoSmithKline. You won’t be billed for the study drugs, hepatitis testing, or the sigmoidoscopy, but will be responsible for standard healthcare costs like co-pays, travel, and follow-up care. You will not be paid for participation. If you experience an injury related to the study, standard medical care is available, but the University of Iowa typically doesn’t cover additional costs unless due to negligence. For questions or concerns, contact Joseph Caster, MD, PhD at 319-356-3693, or the Human Subjects Office at 319-335-6564.

## Side Effects Requiring Immediate Medical Attention:

The side effects listed below require IMMEDIATE MEDICAL ATTENTION OR ADVICE. Call the study doctor right away if you have any of these side effects:

- Allergic reactions can be life-threatening. Symptoms may include difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, low blood pressure (feeling lightheaded, dizziness), tingling around the mouth, rash.
- Rash, itching, blisters, or pale or yellow skin.
- Yellowing of the whites of your eyes or blurry vision.
- Abnormal thinking, confusion, personality changes, headache and/or neck stiffness.
- Diarrhea, stools that are black or bloody, stomach area pain, nausea or vomiting
- Bleeding or bruising more easily than normal. Bleeding may require urgent medical attention, including a transfusion (receiving blood or blood products by vein). Low platelet counts may increase your risk of bleeding and bruising.
- You feel tired, weak, or short of breath. These symptoms may be caused by low red blood cell counts and require a blood transfusion.
- Hair loss or feeling cold.


- Low neutrophil counts may be associated with infection, sometimes severe and life-threatening (neutropenic infection, neutropenic sepsis):
- -Symptoms of severe life-threatening infection may include fever, chills, feeling of low blood pressure (light-headedness, dizziness, fainting), decreased urination, rapid pulse, rapid breathing or shortness of breath
- Decrease in the number of all types of blood cells (pancytopenia)
- Low blood cell counts due to a problem in the bone marrow or blood cancer starting from the bone marrow (Myelodysplastic Syndrome [MDS] or Acute Myeloid Leukemia [AML]). MDS/AML, including fatal cases, have been reported with use of niraparib. If you experience prolonged haematological toxicities, contact your study doctor for haematologist evaluation.
- High blood pressure (hypertension) including severe increase in blood pressure (hypertensive crisis) has been reported with the use of niraparib. If you have pre-existing hypertension, your doctor will determine if your blood pressure is adequately controlled before starting niraparib.
- -Symptoms of a severe increase in blood pressure may include blurry vision, headache, nausea, vomiting, confusion, passing out, seizures, weakness or numbness on one side of body or in one arm or leg and/or difficulty talking (symptoms of a stroke), trouble breathing, chest pain, pain in the upper or lower back, urine that is brown or bloody
- Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES), a rare neurological side effect has been reported with niraparib treatment.

If you have headache, vision changes, confusion or seizure with or without high blood pressure, please contact your doctor.
