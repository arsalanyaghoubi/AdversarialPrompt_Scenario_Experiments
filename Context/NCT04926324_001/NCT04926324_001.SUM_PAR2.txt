Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to determine the best dose of niraparib when combined with radiation and dostarlimab for treating locally advanced rectal cancer. Up to 55 participants will be involved, and your active participation will last approximately 15 weeks, including treatment and follow-up for up to 2 years, and potentially lifelong monitoring. The study involves blood tests, scans (PET/CT, MRI, urinalysis), a flexible sigmoidoscopy, and receiving niraparib, dostarlimab infusions, and radiation therapy. Potential risks include side effects from the drugs and radiation, such as low blood cell counts, infections, high blood pressure, and allergic reactions; serious risks are possible and require immediate medical attention. While there’s no guarantee of benefit, the study may help improve future treatments for rectal cancer. Alternatives include standard radiation therapy alone or other clinical trials. Your participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty. Your medical information will be kept confidential, but may be shared with regulatory agencies and the study sponsor, GlaxoSmithKline. You won’t be billed for the study drugs, hepatitis testing, or the sigmoidoscopy, but will be responsible for standard healthcare costs like co-pays, travel, and follow-up care. You will not be paid for participation. If you experience an injury related to the study, standard medical care is available, but the University of Iowa typically doesn’t cover additional costs unless due to negligence. For questions or concerns, contact Joseph Caster, MD, PhD at 319-356-3693, or the Human Subjects Office at 319-335-6564.

## Before you begin the study treatment

You will need to have some blood tests done to find out if you can continue to be in the study. These tests are part of regular cancer care and would be done even if you do not join the study treatment. If you have had some of them recently, they may not need to be repeated. This is up to your doctor.

If obtained for the study, the results of these tests will be entered into your medical record:

- Blood will be taken to determine blood counts, liver, and kidney function.
- A PET/CT or CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis.
- A pelvic MRI.
- A urinalysis (test of your urine).
- Blood tests to check your bone marrow, liver, and kidney function.
- Blood test to check for hepatitis B and hepatitis C.
- If you inform us you are HIV positive, we will check your CD4 and CD8 lymphocyte count (we will not test for HIV).



- Physical exam with medical history and vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, etc.)
- Pregnancy test (unless you are over 60 years old OR have gone through menopause and had your last menstrual over a year ago).

To participate in this study, you must agree to use an effective method of birth control. You must start using the birth control from the time you sign the consent document and until at least 180 days after your last dose of niraparib. Acceptable methods of birth control include birth control pills, intrauterine device, and abstinence. Condoms and/or diaphragms are not considered effective birth control methods - even with spermicide. Sperm must not be banked/donated for at least 90 days after the last dose of niraparib.

If you have questions about birth control methods, fertility, and/or sperm banking, please speak with your study nurse or your doctors. They will help guide you in these decisions.
