Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to determine if a special imaging scan, called an 18F-fluciclovine PET-CT scan, can better track changes in prostate cancer tumors. You will receive two of these research scans, alongside your usual medical tests. Participation involves an injection of a radioactive agent and a 30-minute scan. While generally safe, potential risks include pain or redness at the injection site, dizziness, or, rarely, allergic reactions. The scan exposes you to radiation, so precautions like limiting contact with pregnant women and infants for two hours after the scan are recommended. There are no direct benefits to you, but future patients may benefit from what we learn. Your participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty. Your medical information will be kept confidential, and study-related scans are provided at no cost to you. If you experience an injury directly related to the study, your medical care will be billed as usual. You won’t be compensated for participation or potential discoveries, and the study may be stopped at any time. Contact Dr. Gregory Ravizzini at 713-745-5721 or the IRB at 713-792-6477 with questions. Your data may be used in future research, with efforts made to remove identifying information.

## 2.  POSSIBLE RISKS

While on this study, you are at risk for side effects. You should discuss these with the study doctor. The known side effects are listed in this form, but they will vary from person to person.

18F-fluciclovine may cause a feeling of warmth or pain at the injection site. It may cause headache and dizziness. It may cause hypersensitivity reactions which may cause breathing and/or skin problems (rash, redness, blisters, itching, and/or local swellings) and may appear either immediately or up to a few days after the injection. It may cause anaphylactic shock (a severe allergic reaction that can cause breathing difficulty and/or a drop in blood pressure).  It may cause changes in the way you move or changes in your senses.

A PET-CT scan may cause you to feel 'closed in' while lying in the scanner. However, the scanner is open at both ends and an intercom allows you to talk with doctors and staff.  If you feel ill or anxious during scanning, doctors and/or technicians will give comfort or the scanning will be stopped.

The PET-CT scan exposes your body to radiation. The radioactive solution does not remain in your system for a long period of time. However, you should wait 2 hours

before holding an infant or getting close to a pregnant woman to avoid exposing them to radiation.  You should drink fluids after the scan to help remove the solution from your system.

This study may involve unpredictable risks to participants.
