Consent Form Summary:

This research study is testing if the drug pembrolizumab can change the immune response within high-risk ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS), a type of non-invasive breast cancer. Approximately 39 patients will participate, receiving injections of pembrolizumab directly into their tumor, followed by standard surgical removal. You may be assigned to a control group receiving standard surgery only. The study involves screenings like blood tests, MRI scans, and physical exams to determine eligibility and monitor your health. Potential side effects are generally mild, such as itching or cough, but more serious, though rare, immune-related reactions are possible. There may be unknown risks as with any new treatment. Participation may not directly benefit your health, but the information gathered could help future patients. You will not be paid for participation, but costs directly related to the research drug and some tests will be covered. Your medical information will be kept private, though legal requirements may necessitate sharing with certain organizations. You can leave the study at any time without penalty. If you experience an injury related to the study, medical treatment will be provided. For questions or concerns, contact Dr. Laura Esserman or Dr. Jasmine Wong at 415-353-7070, or the Committee on Human Research at 415-476-1814. The study duration is approximately 8-15 weeks.

## Screening

This visit will take about 4-5 hours to complete.

The following procedures and assessments will be done during both phases of the study unless otherwise indicated:

-  Ask you about your health and medical history
-  Measure  vital  signs  (blood  pressure,  temperature,  pulse,  breathing  rate,  and  oxygen saturation) including weight and height
-  Conduct a complete physical exam
-  Ask  you  how  well  you  can  do  the  regular  activities  of  daily  life  (called performance status )



-  Ask about any medications (including previous cancer treatments) you have been taking (called concomitant medication )
-  Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) Scan of the breast before being randomized in the dose expansion cohort.
- o An MRI scan takes an image of your body to observe the location and size of your tumor.  For the MRI scan, you may be given a "contrast material" (a special dye that makes it easier for doctors to see different tissues in your body). Gadolinium is contrast material that causes some tumors to appear much brighter than normal tissue on MRI scans (these tumors may not be visible without gadolinium).  The contrast material may be given to you in your arm through an intravenous catheter (a tiny tube inserted into a vein).  You will then lie down on a narrow bed which will be placed in a tunnel that is 6 feet long by 22 inches wide and open at each end.  You will lie there quietly for about one hour, during which time you will hear a loud machine-like noise.  The MRI scan is done in the radiology department and takes approximately an hour and a half to complete.
-  Collect urine samples to check for safety factors including urine pregnancy test. Results need to be within 3 day prior to beginning study treatment.
-  Collect blood samples (about 2 tablespoons) for:
1. Routine safety tests
- o Measuring the chemical parts of your blood (called biochemistry )
- o Counting the number of white and red blood cells and platelets (called hematology )
2. Pregnancy test, if you are a woman of childbearing potential and the urine pregnancy test cannot confirm a negative result. Results need to be within 3 days prior to beginning study treatment.
3. Seeing how well your blood clots (called coagulation )
4. Seeing how well your thyroid is working (called thyroid function test )
5. Research
