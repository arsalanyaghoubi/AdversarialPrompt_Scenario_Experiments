Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to learn more about combining immunotherapy – a treatment to help your body fight cancer – with a type of radiation treatment called yttrium-90 radioembolization (Y90-RE) for colorectal cancer that has spread to the liver. Approximately 18 people at the University of Iowa will participate over about 2 years, receiving durvalumab immunotherapy alongside Y90-RE. You’ll have clinic visits and treatments over up to 10 weeks, each lasting 4-6 hours, and a tumor biopsy may be taken. Potential risks include side effects from the treatments, like skin changes or fatigue, and the possibility of unknown risks. While there’s no guarantee of benefit, the study hopes to improve future treatments. You can choose to stop participating at any time without penalty. Your health information will be kept confidential, but may be shared with regulatory agencies and study sponsors. Medical care for any study-related injury will be available, but you’ll generally be responsible for standard medical costs. Participation is voluntary, and you’ll receive no payment, but the study covers the cost of the immunotherapy drug and investigational device. If significant new findings emerge, you will be informed. For questions or concerns, contact Dr. Chandrikha Chandrasekharan at (319) 356-2425 or the Human Subjects Office at (319) 335-6564.

## WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF THIS STUDY?

This is a research study. We are inviting you to participate in this research study because you have been
diagnosed with metastatic colorectal cancer with liver metastases.

The purpose of this research study is to find out more about the side effects of **immunotherapy** with a
form of **radiation** treatment for the cancer in the liver called **yttrium-90 radioembolization (Y90-RE)** .
Radioembolization is a minimally invasive procedure where beads are filled with radioactive isotype Y90 drug and then are injected in the blood vessels that feed the tumor. Y90-RE is one of the therapies
used to treat patients with liver cancer.  The immunotherapy drug is called durvalumab. You may
receive 1-5 doses of the drug up to every 2 weeks to determine how many durvalumab infusions are
tolerable.

We are trying to study what doses of durvalumab are safe for people in combination with this form of
radiation treatment. Everyone in this study will receive durvalumab, which is still experimental and
isn’t approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for metastatic colorectal cancer.
However, the FDA has allowed the use of this drug in this research study. Microscopic radioactive
particles (TheraSphere®) will be used for radioembolization to deliver the Y90 drug to your liver.
TheraSphere® is considered investigational, which means that it has not been approved by the U.S.
Food and Drug Administration.

The immunotherapy drug is not new and has been approved for other cancers, e.g. bladder cancer and
lung cancer. We don’t know all the ways that this drug may affect people. The primary goal of this
study is to assess safety. We also want to know if combining these two kinds of treatments will help




fight your cancer.

It is important to note that prior studies of immunotherapy treatments when given alone have not shown
any benefit for patients with metastatic colorectal cancer. The immunotherapy drug durvalumab,
therefore, is being combined with the Y90 radiation to see if these two treatments will help each other.
We still don’t know if the study is likely to help you, but we hope the information from this study will
help us develop a better treatment for metastatic colorectal cancer with liver metastasis in the future.
