Consent Form Summary:

This research study aims to determine if a combination of VB-111 and nivolumab is safe and can shrink tumors in patients with colorectal cancer that has spread, specifically those with a stable genetic marker (MSS). If you join, you’ll undergo tests to confirm eligibility, including blood work and scans, and may have tumor biopsies to assess treatment effects. Treatment involves receiving VB-111 and nivolumab through an IV every two weeks, with regular check-ups at the NIH Clinical Center. Common side effects of VB-111 include flu-like symptoms, while nivolumab can cause effects like fatigue or intestinal issues. Standard care options like chemotherapy are available if you choose not to participate. Your participation is voluntary, and you can withdraw at any time without penalty. Your medical information will be kept private, though shared with the study team and potentially other researchers with identifiers removed. The NIH will cover some expenses related to the study. While there’s no guarantee of benefit, the study could help others in the future. If you experience a research-related injury, short-term medical care will be provided. For questions or concerns, contact Dr. Tim Greten at 240-760-6114 or gretentf@mail.nih.gov.
